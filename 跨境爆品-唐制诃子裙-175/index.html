Craft notes

Thread,
cut, cloth.

Three things carry this piece: a Tang-dynasty construction, an embroidered bodice worked with a figural motif, and an ice-porcelain cotton blend chosen for warm weather.

01

Hezi bodice

诃子 · 唐制

The hezi is a high, strapless bodice associated with Tang-dynasty dress, worn over a skirt and often beneath a wide-sleeve robe. Here it is embroidered with a floral, figural motif in apricot, gold and blue thread.

02

Wide sleeves

广袖

The robe falls open at the front with generous 广袖 sleeves that drape past the hand. Sheer outer fabric keeps the volume without the weight — it moves, it does not hang.

03

Ice-porcelain cotton

冰瓷棉

A cotton-and-polyester blend with a cool, smooth hand, used here in a summer-weight listing. Two fabric components are specified at 90–95% and 70–80% content respectively.

Questions

Before you buy.

Is this a costume or everyday clothing?

It is hanfu: a reconstructed Tang-dynasty silhouette. The listing describes it as suitable for daily wear and summer, and the light cotton blend supports that — but the wide sleeves and pleated skirt read as occasion wear in most cities.

What is a hezi skirt?

A hezi is a high bodice worn over a skirt, associated with Tang-dynasty dress. This set pairs an embroidered hezi with a pleated skirt and a wide-sleeve outer robe.

What is the fabric?

An ice-porcelain cotton and polyester blend. The supplier specifies two components at 90–95% and 70–80% content; no silk is listed.

Will the color look like the photos?

The photographs are the supplier’s own, shot outdoors and on a white background. Monitors shift green and pink more than most hues; treat the studio frame in the gallery as the closest reference.

How should I care for it?

Cool hand wash, dry in shade, low iron on the reverse. Keep the embroidered bodice away from a hot iron face.

Where does it ship from?

From the Huachaoji atelier in China, through the cross-border listing. Duties and delivery windows are confirmed at checkout and are not set on this page.
